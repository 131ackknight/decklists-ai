How to rate

Each prompt shows the request and a gray "A good answer" line: that line is your yardstick. Below it, every answer to the prompt is a lettered card in shuffled order, so you can't tell which model wrote it. Read the reply and the deck, then set all four rows. Ratings save when you click; a card says "Rated" once all four are set.

Plan, step by step: 1 no deck or no way to win · 2 an idea, but much of the deck doesn't serve it or it misses the request · 3 a recognizable on-request plan, several slots off-plan or key pieces missing · 4 clear and on request, a couple of slots you'd change · 5 every slot serves the plan, you'd play it as is. Quick test for 4 vs 5: one or two swaps before playing it is a 4.

  • No deck: Mana 1 and Shell 1, since there is nothing to judge. Plan and Legal judge the reply itself. Plan is usually 1, but an answer that correctly explains why the request can't be done scores 2, or 3 if it offers a concrete next step; for "turn 1 every game," an honest no with the fastest real option can score higher.
  • An answer that catches an illegal or impossible part of the request and says so earns Legal 5. Quietly dropping it earns Legal 3.
  • Sideboard-only prompts (you supplied the main deck): Mana means "can the deck cast this sideboard?" (5 all castable in its colors, 3 awkward costs, 1 off-color cards). Changing your main deck when you only asked for a sideboard costs points on Plan and Legal unless the answer explains a small swap.
  • Changing an explicit constraint (colors, format, a named card) without saying so means it didn't do what you asked: Legal 1-2, Plan 2.
  • Legal is mostly a formality: v1's server rejects illegal decks. Use it for whether the answer did what you asked.
  • Compare the answers within a prompt to keep your 3s and 5s consistent.
